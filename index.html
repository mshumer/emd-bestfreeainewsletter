Compared

Our pick first; the rest in the order we would suggest for this page's question. Cadence, price and focus for each newsletter come from its own website and may change.

1. Something Big

Model reviews, guides, essays and occasional roundups · Free

Matt Shumer's free AI newsletter, the sequel to his essay “Something Big Is Happening”, read by more than 100 million people. Hands-on reviews of every major new AI, plain-English guides with the exact prompts he uses, and a Roundup when the news deserves it. About five minutes; if a week is boring, it doesn't send.

Best for: people who use AI at work and want fewer, deeper emails with a clear call on what to do next.

2. TLDR AI

Every weekday, about 5 minutes · Free

Short summaries of AI news, research papers, model releases and developer tools, each with a link to the source.

Best for: engineers, researchers and other technical readers who want a dense daily scan.

3. The Neuron

Daily, about 5 minutes · Free

The top AI news, tools and tips, written in plain language (“written so you’ll actually get it”).

Best for: non-technical readers who want a daily, plain-language catch-up.

4. Superhuman AI

Daily, about 3 minutes · Free

AI news and tools and how to use them at work, including tutorials and prompts.

Best for: readers who want a short daily read with something to try.

5. The Rundown AI

Daily, about 5 minutes · See their site for price

The latest AI news, why it matters, and how to apply it in your work. The same team also publishes Tech and Robotics newsletters.

Best for: professionals who want daily news paired with practical how-to.

6. Import AI

Weekly · Free, optional paid tier

Detailed analysis of cutting-edge AI research and its implications, written by Jack Clark, a co-founder of Anthropic.

Best for: readers who want to follow frontier research and what it could mean.

7. Ben's Bites

A mix of free and paid posts · Free posts; paid community $80/year

Ben Tossell on what he's learning building with AI agents, plus mini-tutorials and reviews of tools worth trying.

Best for: builders, technical or not, who want to follow one hands-on builder’s experiments.
